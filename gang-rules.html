Gang Regulations

Rules governing criminal groups, recognised gangs, organised hostilities and gang-related roleplay throughout Ash Creek.

These regulations are out-of-character server rules. Their contents do not automatically represent information known by any character.

Chapter I

Groups and Official Recognition

1.1

Informal Groups

Players may form informal groups, travel together and participate in criminal roleplay without registering as an official gang.

1.2

Freedom to Roam

Informal groups may roam throughout Ash Creek, build relationships and develop their identity naturally through roleplay.

1.3

Limits on Informal Groups

An informal group is not considered an officially recognised gang. It may not participate in a declared gang war, claim access to recognised-gang privileges or represent itself as staff-approved.

1.4

Recognition Ticket

A group seeking official recognition must open a support ticket and provide the information requested by server staff.

1.5

Recognition Is Not Automatic

Creating a ticket does not guarantee approval. Staff may consider the group's activity, roleplay quality, leadership, history and suitability before granting recognition.

1.6

Roleplay Before Recognition

Groups should establish themselves through meaningful in-character roleplay. Recognition is intended to acknowledge an existing group, not create one without prior development.

1.7

Official Gang Wars

Only officially recognised gangs may enter a formal gang war. Both participating groups must satisfy any staff requirements before hostilities are treated as a declared war.

Chapter II

The Rule of Five

2.1

Maximum Participation

No more than five players from the same group, gang or cooperating side may actively participate in a criminal activity or hostile situation.

2.2

Criminal Activities

The Rule of Five applies to robberies, kidnappings, ambushes, raids, organised theft, extortion, violent confrontations and other coordinated criminal scenes.

2.3

Hostilities

The Rule of Five also applies to gang disputes, retaliations, planned attacks, declared wars and any other organised hostility between groups.

2.4

Nearby Members

Additional members may not remain nearby to act as scouts, lookouts, reserves, medics, transport or reinforcements when doing so provides an advantage to the active group.

2.5

Reinforcements

Players may not rotate in and out of a scene to avoid the participant limit. Once five members are involved, no additional member may join that continuing situation.

2.6

Splitting the Group

A gang may not deliberately divide into multiple teams to attack the same target, location or opposing group as separate incidents.

2.7

Allied Participants

Allied gangs, associates and hired assistance count toward the same limit. Two groups may not combine their separate limits during one activity.

2.8

Staff Events

Staff may authorise a different participant limit for a planned server event. Unless this permission is clearly provided beforehand, the Rule of Five remains in effect.

Chapter III

Membership and Leadership

3.1

Leader Responsibility

Gang leaders are responsible for setting expectations, managing their members and addressing behaviour that damages the quality or fairness of roleplay.

3.2

Individual Responsibility

Every player remains responsible for their own conduct. A member may not excuse a rule breach by claiming that they were following an order from gang leadership.

3.3

Meaningful Membership

Gang membership must be established through roleplay. Players should not be added solely to increase numbers before a robbery, conflict or other major criminal activity.

3.4

Temporary Members

Gangs may not use temporary recruits, unnamed associates or sudden alliances to bypass participation limits or gain an advantage.

3.5

Character Loyalty

Joining, leaving or betraying a gang should carry appropriate in-character weight. Players should avoid repeatedly changing groups without meaningful character development.

3.6

Identifiers

Clothing, colours, horses or accessories may contribute to a gang's identity, but they do not automatically prove that a character belongs to that gang.

Chapter IV

Territory and Influence

4.1

Roleplayed Influence

Territorial influence must be established and maintained through roleplay. It is not granted merely because a gang frequently occupies a location.

4.2

Public Locations

A gang may not permanently prevent other players from using a town, road, business or other publicly accessible area.

4.3

No Automatic Knowledge

Players may not assume that a location belongs to a gang unless that information has been learned through legitimate in-character means.

4.4

Businesses and Civilians

Territorial roleplay must not be used to continuously harass civilians, monopolise businesses or deny others reasonable access to ordinary roleplay.

4.5

Disputed Territory

Territorial disputes should develop gradually and provide both sides with opportunities for negotiation, response and continuing story.

Chapter V

Conflict and Gang Wars

5.1

Meaningful Cause

Gang conflict must arise from meaningful in-character events. Minor disagreements should not be used as an excuse for immediate or excessive violence.

5.2

Escalation

Hostilities should escalate naturally through roleplay. Negotiation, warnings, threats and other proportionate responses should be considered before repeated lethal confrontations.

5.3

Declaration of War

A formal gang war may only occur between officially recognised gangs and must be reported through the appropriate staff ticket process.

5.4

Terms of Conflict

Staff may require both gangs to establish clear terms, objectives, limitations and conditions for ending the war before formal hostilities begin.

5.5

Rule of Five During War

A declared war does not remove the Rule of Five. Unless staff explicitly authorise otherwise, each hostile encounter remains limited to five active participants on either side.

5.6

Repeated Attacks

Players must not repeatedly target the same gang, individual or location without allowing reasonable time and roleplay between encounters.

5.7

Third-Party Interference

Uninvolved groups may not enter an active gang conflict merely to benefit one side, obtain easy violence or change the outcome of an encounter.

5.8

Ending Hostilities

Peace agreements, surrender terms and the conclusion of a gang war must be respected. A concluded conflict may not be immediately restarted without new and meaningful roleplay.

Chapter VI

Alliances and Outside Assistance

6.1

Roleplayed Alliances

Alliances must be formed through legitimate in-character interaction and must make sense for the characters and groups involved.

6.2

No Limit Stacking

Allied groups do not receive separate participant allowances. All cooperating players count toward the same Rule of Five.

6.3

Outside Gunmen

Players may not hire or recruit outside gunmen for the purpose of bypassing gang limits, membership expectations or war restrictions.

6.4

Mutual Defence

An alliance does not create unrestricted permission to join every dispute involving the allied group. Assistance must remain reasonable and comply with all participation limits.

6.5

Oversized Coalitions

Alliances may not be used to create a permanent oversized criminal organisation while presenting its members as separate gangs.

Chapter VII

Hostages, Robberies and Criminal Scenes

7.1

Roleplay Before Reward

Robberies and hostage situations should create enjoyable roleplay for everyone involved. Winning, escaping or obtaining money should not be treated as more important than the scene itself.

7.2

Genuine Hostages

Hostages should be genuine participants in the scene. Friends, gang members or cooperating players may not be presented as unwilling hostages to obtain an advantage.

7.3

Hostage Treatment

Hostages must be given reasonable opportunities to roleplay. They should not be subjected to unnecessary humiliation, prolonged confinement or behaviour intended only to upset the player.

7.4

Reasonable Demands

Demands should be proportionate to the scene. Players should not make impossible demands solely to force negotiations to fail.

7.5

Scene Participation

Every gang member actively assisting with a robbery, escape, lookout position or hostage operation counts toward the Rule of Five.

7.6

Interruption of Scenes

Other gangs should not interfere with an active robbery or hostage scene unless they have a clear, immediate and legitimate in-character reason.

Chapter VIII

Information and Metagaming

8.1

Character Knowledge

Information learned through Discord, streams, websites, tickets or out-of-character conversation may not be used by a character unless it has also been learned through roleplay.

8.2

Gang Identity

A character may not identify a gang member solely because the player recognises their voice, clothing, username, horse or usual companions.

8.3

Restricted Records

The absence or presence of information on this website does not grant in-character knowledge concerning any gang, member, territory or conflict.

8.4

External Communication

Players may not use private messages, streams or external voice channels to coordinate an active in-character criminal scene.

8.5

Stream Sniping

Viewing another player's stream to discover their location, plans, associates or current activity is prohibited.

Chapter IX

Administration and Enforcement

9.1

Leadership Changes

Recognised gangs must report significant leadership changes through the appropriate ticket system.

9.2

Material Changes

Major changes to a gang's name, identity, leadership or structure may require staff review.

9.3

Inactivity

A gang that becomes inactive for an extended period may have its official recognition reviewed or withdrawn.

9.4

Loss of Recognition

Staff may withdraw recognition where a gang repeatedly breaches server rules, fails to maintain suitable leadership or no longer operates as an active roleplay group.

9.5

Combat Logging

Players may not disconnect, respawn or otherwise leave the game to avoid the consequences of an active criminal or hostile situation.

9.6

Reporting Concerns

Rule concerns should be reported through the proper support process. Players should not pause or derail an active scene to argue about rules unless immediate staff intervention is necessary.

9.7

Staff Discretion

Staff may intervene where conduct undermines fair roleplay, even where a specific situation is not described word-for-word within these regulations.

Final Notice

Roleplay Comes First

Gang roleplay should create continuing stories rather than repeated conflict without consequence. All groups are expected to consider fairness, escalation, character development and the enjoyment of every player involved.
